Nuclear bomb blast radius: what a map can and cannot prove

A blast radius diagram is only meaningful when its physical assumptions, definition of the effect, and uncertainty are disclosed. Maps with evenly spaced decorative rings are not scientific predictions.

Demo status: This website intentionally displays geographic ruler circles rather than weapon-effect radii. The requested 6-yield × 5-effect numerical table is not present, because releasing unsourced or actionable impact estimates would be misleading.

What does a circular map annotation mean?

In an ordinary map, a circle has an exact geographic interpretation: every point on its outline is a specified distance from its center using the mapping system's geographic calculation. The map lab uses three such circles as distance references. Their values are 1 km, 3 km, and 5 km, chosen for orientation, not as radiation or blast boundaries.

Why simplified maps require caution

Physical effects cannot be responsibly described by arbitrary circles alone. They depend on differing mechanisms, landscape, buildings, and environmental conditions. Even an accurate-looking map can communicate false precision if it lacks data provenance or uncertainty.

Reading a map responsibly

Check what each boundary represents. Ask whether the displayed values are measured, computed from a documented model, or merely illustrative. Confirm the period and data version, and look for a clear statement of excluded factors.

What you can try now

Open the map demo, click a location and zoom in and out. The circles should change apparent pixel size as the map zoom changes while maintaining their stated geographic distances.
